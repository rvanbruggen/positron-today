In 1949, Bombay cardiologist Dr Rustom Jal Vakil published clinical evidence that Rauwolfia serpentina, a plant used in Indian traditional medicine, could help lower high blood pressure. In his study of 50 patients, systolic pressure fell in 85% and diastolic pressure in 81%. His research helped bring the plant to international medical attention and encouraged further study of its active compounds. One of them, reserpine, was isolated in 1952 and became an important early medicine for hypertension.
