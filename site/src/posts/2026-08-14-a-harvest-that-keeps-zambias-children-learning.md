In southern Zambia, families are using income from cotton to support their children's education, buying uniforms and books while putting food on the table. The introduction of free education has significantly increased enrollment, but many children still face barriers due to additional costs. Cotton farming is seen as a vital lifeline, especially during droughts when other crops fail. Farmers like Benita Mainga highlight how this cash crop directly contributes to their children's schooling, making it essential for rural families.
