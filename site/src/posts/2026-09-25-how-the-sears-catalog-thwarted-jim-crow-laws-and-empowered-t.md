I find it fascinating how the Sears catalog, a simple mail-order book, quietly challenged Jim Crow laws by giving Black Americans a way to shop without facing discrimination. Before Sears, shopping meant humiliation and unfair treatment, but the catalog offered anonymity and fair prices, empowering Black communities economically. This shift helped teach a 'language of rights' that fueled the civil rights movement long before the famous sit-ins. Even when local businesses tried to fight back, Sears found clever ways to keep serving its customers, showing how business and social change can unexpectedly connect.
