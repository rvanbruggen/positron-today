So: in 1997, ecologists arranged for a juice company to spread 12,000 metric tons of orange peels and pulp across a worn-out pasture in Costa Rica. Sixteen years later, researchers returned and found the site transformed, with dense vegetation so thick they couldn’t even spot the sign. Compared with nearby pastureland, the three-hectare plot had 176% more aboveground biomass, richer soil and around two dozen plant species. It’s a striking example of how food waste can help tropical forests regrow and absorb carbon.
