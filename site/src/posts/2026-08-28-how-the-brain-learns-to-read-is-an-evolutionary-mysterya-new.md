Isn't it fascinating how our brains learn to read? Despite reading being a relatively new skill, our brains adapt existing systems to make sense of written words. A recent study sheds light on the brain structures involved in reading and how they differ among individuals, including those with dyslexia. It turns out that our auditory abilities play a crucial role in this process, and genetic factors might influence how easily we learn to read. This research opens up exciting possibilities for understanding literacy across diverse populations.
