For the first time, scientists have pinpointed the age of prehistoric paintings in Font-de-Gaume, a stunning cave in France. They discovered that the black images of a bison and a mask were created during different periods, which is a breakthrough for understanding when humans first entered these decorated caves. Using advanced techniques, researchers found traces of charcoal in the pigments, allowing for accurate radiocarbon dating. This exciting discovery opens new doors for archaeologists to explore the history of cave art and its creators.
