A squeeze of lemon can brighten a dish, and scientists are now uncovering how sourness shapes what we taste. In a study of mice, researchers found that sour-sensing cells in taste buds release serotonin. This chemical can dampen nearby signals for other tastes, including sweetness and bitterness, before taste information reaches the brain. So taste buds do more than detect flavors - they actively help shape the experience, and researchers hope to trace the pathways that carry those signals onward.
