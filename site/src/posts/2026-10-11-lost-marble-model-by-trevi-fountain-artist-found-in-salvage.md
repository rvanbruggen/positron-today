What a remarkable rediscovery: a marble model by Pietro Bracci, one of eighteenth-century Rome’s great sculptors, turned up among garden ornaments in a Wiltshire salvage yard. Bracci created the 68-centimetre figure of Pope Clement XII around the 1730s as a proposal for a giant bronze statue, which was later melted down for scrap. A builder spotted the model’s quality and bought it for just £300. Now, this rare survivor is heading to auction, with its value estimated at up to $13,300.
