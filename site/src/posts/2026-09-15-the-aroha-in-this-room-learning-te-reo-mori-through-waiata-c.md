Every weeknight in Auckland, a diverse group of people - Pākehā, migrants, and Māori reconnecting with their heritage - gather to learn te reo Māori through waiata, the traditional songs that carry language, culture, and local stories. For learner Cameron Grant, the class has become a sanctuary where he feels peace and aroha, while also giving him confidence to share the beauty of Māori culture with others. Teacher Eda Tang, a New Zealand-born Chinese educator, brings her own experience of language loss and cultural connection to the class, viewing te reo Māori revitalisation as a vital kaupapa that honors Te Tiriti and the mana of Aotearoa. The waiata class works because it's never been about perfection or performance - it's about people genuinely connecting with te reo and te ao Māori, whether they're Italian immigrants or locals seeking deeper roots in the place they call home.
