So: while many cities are only now embracing pedestrian-first planning, Pontevedra in Spain began reclaiming its streets back in 1999. Mayor Miguel Anxo Fernández Lores gradually restricted unnecessary cars, removed parking and made 300,000 square meters of the city center pedestrian-friendly. Roads were narrowed, sidewalks widened and speed limits lowered, helping residents take back public spaces from traffic. Today, Pontevedra inspires city leaders around the world with an approach linked to cleaner air, less noise and a more livable city.
