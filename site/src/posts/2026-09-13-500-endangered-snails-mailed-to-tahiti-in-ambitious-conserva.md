The Detroit Zoo has bred and shipped 500 Partula snails overnight to Tahiti, where they'll be released into the wild to restore native populations that nearly vanished due to an introduced predatory snail species. Each snail sports an orange UV-glowing dot that identifies them as part of the 2026 release cohort, allowing conservationists to track their progress in their natural habitat. This effort is part of a larger international recovery program that has already returned over 17,000 individual snails of 11 different Partula species to French Polynesia, with some species even producing wild-born offspring. What makes this project genuinely exciting is that it shows how zoos can work together across continents - the snails stop over at St. Louis Zoo for special dietary prep before their final journey - to bring species back from the brink of extinction.
