So, Seattle just joined thousands of school districts offering free meals to every student regardless of income - and it's become a real national trend since 2016. Here's the thing: when researchers looked at the data, they found these programs actually do boost test scores, with math and English improvements ranging from small to moderate depending on which students we're talking about. Now, not everyone agrees on the impact - some studies suggest the benefits are pretty minimal - but experts like Amy Ellen Schwartz from the University of Delaware call it "the best thing since sliced bread" because it's surprisingly cost-effective compared to other school interventions. And which brings me to the broader picture: these programs help with food insecurity and attendance too, making them a win across multiple fronts even if the academic gains are sometimes subtle.
