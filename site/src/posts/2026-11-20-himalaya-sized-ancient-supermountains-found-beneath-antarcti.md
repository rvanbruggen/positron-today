So, scientists have uncovered the buried roots of ancient mountain ranges beneath Antarctica’s ice, once comparable in scale to the Himalayas. The ranges formed between 650 and 450 million years ago as Antarctica collided with India and Africa during the assembly of Gondwana. By analysing 1,712 zircon grains from ocean-floor sediments, researchers reconstructed the mountains’ age and geological origins without drilling through the ice. Their erosion may have enriched the oceans, reduced atmospheric carbon dioxide and helped create conditions for the first hard-shelled organisms and the Cambrian explosion.
