So, a beautiful Tamil doorstep tradition also holds a precise set of rules that computers can understand. In the 1970s, professor Gift Siromoney and his wife, Rani, documented how women built kolams around grids of dots, recording each stage with layered paper and carbon copies. They described the patterns as a visual language whose rules could be used to generate designs - and whose ideas even connect with research on circular DNA. And here's the thing: a daily folk art helped inspire new ways for computers to draw and recognise images.
