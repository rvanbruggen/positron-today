Researchers in Denmark and Luxembourg have created a tiny, frog-shaped robot that swims without a motor, battery or wires. Its six artificial muscles contract when warmed by near-infrared light, pushing water backwards and propelling the robot forwards at about four centimetres per minute. And here's the thing: the team printed its soft materials together, refining how the muscles work with the silicone body. The findings could help guide future soft robots for environmental monitoring or search-and-rescue missions, though they still need a power source independent of external light.
