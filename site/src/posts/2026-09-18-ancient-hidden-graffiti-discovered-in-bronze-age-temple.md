So here's what's fascinating - archaeologists using high-resolution laser scans have uncovered over 40 ship images hidden in plain sight at a Bronze Age temple in Cyprus for more than 40 years. The vessels, etched into the walls of Kition Kathari, were practically invisible to the naked eye, which explains why they went unnoticed even during earlier excavations in the 1980s. What really gets me excited is that these weren't created by a single artist - contextual evidence shows the ships were carved over a 300-year span, suggesting continuous maritime culture through major historical changes. The research reveals six distinct categories of ship designs, including Levantine, Minoan, and hybrid galleys from various Mediterranean regions, which challenges old assumptions about the Bronze Age collapse and cultural continuity.
