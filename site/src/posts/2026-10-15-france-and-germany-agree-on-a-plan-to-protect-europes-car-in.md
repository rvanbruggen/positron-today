So: France and Germany have reached a provisional deal aimed at protecting Europe’s car industry, pairing more flexibility on emissions with a stronger “Made in Europe” approach. Under the proposal, carmakers would need to cut CO2 emissions by 90% by 2035, with biofuels among the options cited for the remaining emissions. In return, Germany backs stricter European sourcing rules for companies receiving public funds, applying from 2028 and including close partners such as Britain, Norway and Switzerland. The agreement still needs approval from Germany’s Social Democrats, but it could help move the EU’s automotive plans forward.
