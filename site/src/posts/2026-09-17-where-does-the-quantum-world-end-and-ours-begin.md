So here's the thing - quantum mechanics is tested to an incredible precision, yet we still don't have one clear interpretation of what it actually means for reality. In this fascinating podcast conversation, Jonathan Halliwell from Imperial College London explains how decoherence - this process where quantum systems lose their fragile wave-like behavior through interactions with the environment - is the key bridge between the quantum and classical worlds. He tackles some of the field's deepest paradoxes, including whether we really need a conscious observer for quantum collapse and whether Einstein was right about the moon existing when nobody's looking. And here's what I find beautiful - Halliwell uses yoga and meditation to make peace with the mystery of competing scientific perspectives, showing us that sometimes sitting with uncertainty is part of the journey.
