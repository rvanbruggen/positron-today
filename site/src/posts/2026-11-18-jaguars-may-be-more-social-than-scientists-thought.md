So: after observing jaguars in Brazil’s Pantanal for 13 years, researchers found that these famously independent cats sometimes form lasting partnerships. A few males patrolled, ate, and rested together, with two partnerships lasting more than seven years. The 69 jaguars studied also had many more peaceful encounters than hostile ones, especially where food was plentiful. Jaguars still spend much of their lives alone, but this long-term study shows their social lives are more nuanced than we once believed.
