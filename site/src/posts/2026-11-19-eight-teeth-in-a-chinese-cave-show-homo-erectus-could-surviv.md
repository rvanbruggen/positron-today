So: eight teeth found in two caves in southern China suggest that Homo erectus lived in tropical rainforest between 324,000 and 184,000 years ago. Fossils of forest elephants, orangutans and rhinos, along with evidence from the teeth, help paint a picture of this ancient habitat. The findings point to a more adaptable human relative than researchers had assumed, although the rainforest evidence relies on carbon isotopes from just three teeth and the labels used for ancient human groups remain debated. And here's the intriguing possibility: another human tooth from the same caves may belong to a Denisovan, raising the prospect that the two groups crossed paths.
