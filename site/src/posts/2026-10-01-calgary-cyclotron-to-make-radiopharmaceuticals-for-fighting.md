So: Calgary is gearing up to build its very first cyclotron, a device that speeds up particles to create radioactive materials used in cancer diagnosis and treatment. This new facility, set to open by 2029, will cut down the travel time for these short-lived medicines, making treatment faster and more reliable for patients in southern Alberta. The project is part of a $71 million investment, combining government and donor funds, to boost cancer care in the region. And here's the thing - with cancer treatments evolving rapidly, this cyclotron will support cutting-edge therapies like theranostics, which use radioactive tracers to track and treat cancer internally.
