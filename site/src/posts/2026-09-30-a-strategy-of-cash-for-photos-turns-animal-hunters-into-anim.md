So: on the magical island of Borneo, a clever program called KehatiKu is paying villagers to snap photos and record sounds of wildlife instead of hunting them. This cash-for-photos idea has turned hunters into protectors, with locals earning money by capturing images of rare species like orangutans and flat-headed cats. It’s fascinating how a simple shift in incentives can lead to over 378,000 confirmed wildlife observations and a real boost in conservation efforts. And here's the thing, this program not only helps protect endangered animals but also supports local families with extra income.
