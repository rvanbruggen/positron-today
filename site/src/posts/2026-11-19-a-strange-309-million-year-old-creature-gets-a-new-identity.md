An exceptionally preserved fossil from Illinois has revealed Jeanerpeton mazonensis, a previously unknown animal that lived 309 million years ago, long before dinosaurs. Resembling a stocky, short-bodied salamander, it retains striking details including scales, tiny belly ribs and impressions of its eyes. Researchers re-examined a specimen once classified as Amphibamus grandiceps and found enough anatomical differences to identify a distinct species, described in the Journal of Vertebrate Paleontology. Its name means “Jean’s crawler from Mazon Creek,” honoring the mothers of the two student scientists who helped uncover its identity - a lovely reminder that museum collections can still surprise us.
