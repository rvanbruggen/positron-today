So, researchers set out to learn what happens to the landscape after an elephant dies, and their findings revealed an impressive afterlife. At carcass sites in South Africa’s Kruger National Park, soil held far higher levels of important nutrients, while the initial surge gradually gave way to rich grass growth. Grazing animals visited these areas up to three times more often, and the effects could last as long as eight years. It’s a striking reminder that elephants continue to support their ecosystems even after they’re gone.
