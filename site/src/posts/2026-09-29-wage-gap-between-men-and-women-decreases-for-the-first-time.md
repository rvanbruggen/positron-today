So: the wage gap between men and women in Denmark shrank last year for the first time in three years, dropping from 12.9% to 12.3%. That’s great news, especially since wages directly affect pension savings, which means this change could lead to more economic equality for retirees. Experts like Mads Moberg Reumert and Helle Dalsgaard see this as a positive step, even if women still earn significantly less overall. And here’s the thing: with more women gaining higher education and men taking more parental leave, the outlook for closing the gap looks promising.
