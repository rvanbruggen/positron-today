So here's what fascinated me about this research - historian Bram Hilkens spent seven years digging into whether economic growth automatically means more inequality, using seventeenth-century Holland as his laboratory. He discovered something really interesting through old land registers called morgenboeken: as the Dutch economy boomed during the Golden Age, more and more ordinary people could actually afford to buy little plots of land. Now, Hilkens is careful to put "Golden Age" in quotes - he doesn't ignore how colonial exploitation was the dark side of that prosperity - but his data shows something nuanced about how wealth spread during this period. What strikes me is how he moved from studying Kendrick Lamar's music to analyzing centuries-old property records, proving that curiosity about inequality knows no era.
