The Simons Observatory in Chile is turning its instruments toward the oldest light in the cosmos: the cosmic microwave background, a faint afterglow from the Big Bang. By mapping tiny variations in that light, researchers hope to learn how the universe expanded and evolved in its earliest moments. Its sensitive telescopes are designed to reveal subtle signals that earlier observations could not resolve as clearly. And here's the thing: a sharper view of this ancient glow could bring scientists closer to answering some of cosmology's biggest questions.
