Here’s an encouraging finding: people born later in the twentieth century entered old age with stronger memory and, on average, experienced slower decline than earlier generations. Researchers tracked memory-test results from 219,000 adults aged 50 and older across three decades, finding that each ten-year shift in birth decade was linked to a 10% higher score at age 60. They also found that each successive birth decade was associated with a 64% greater likelihood of keeping memory stable, while brain scans linked stable memory with less shrinkage in the hippocampus. So, improvements in education, health and lifestyle earlier in life may help explain why dementia risk at a given age has fallen, even as populations grow older.
