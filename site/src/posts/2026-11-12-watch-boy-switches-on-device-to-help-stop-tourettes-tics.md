A wrist device developed by researchers at the University of Nottingham is offering people with Tourette syndrome a new way to manage their tics. It delivers gentle electrical pulses to a nerve in the wrist, and trials suggest this can affect brain activity involved in controlling movement. Fifteen-year-old Ollie, from Cheshire, began experiencing involuntary neck movements nearly two years ago and is among those using the device. The report captures what he describes as a life-changing moment when he switches it on.
