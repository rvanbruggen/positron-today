The Antarctic blue whale population has grown from 418 in 1973 to an estimated 4,018 today, a remarkable recovery since whaling ended. Researchers pieced together historical catch records, photo identifications and decades of surveys to track the whales’ return. These slow-reproducing giants are still considered critically endangered, but their recovery offers real hope. And because blue whales help shape marine ecosystems, their comeback benefits many other creatures too.
