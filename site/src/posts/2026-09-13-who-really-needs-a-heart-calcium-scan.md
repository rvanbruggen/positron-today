Coronary artery calcium scans are gaining popularity for assessing heart disease risk, but new research suggests they may not be necessary for everyone. A study followed over 6,000 adults and found that the scans mainly benefit those in borderline risk categories. For these patients, the calcium score provides clearer insights into their actual risk of cardiovascular events. However, using the scans on low-risk individuals could lead to unnecessary costs and radiation exposure.
