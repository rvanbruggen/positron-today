So, deep-sea sea pigs are hiding a remarkable resident inside their body walls: a newly identified bacterium named Candidatus Abyssoplasma scotoplanesicola. Its genome is remarkably small, with just 649,000 DNA bases, yet more than a quarter of it is devoted to genes for surface proteins. Some of these proteins are enormous, stretching beyond 5,000 amino acids, and may help the bacteria interact with their hosts or gather in clusters. The discovery offers a fascinating new glimpse into how bacteria adapt to life closely connected with other organisms.
