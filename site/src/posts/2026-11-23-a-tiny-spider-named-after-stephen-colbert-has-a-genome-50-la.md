A tiny California trapdoor spider named for Stephen Colbert has a genome about 50% larger than a human’s, despite being only about the size of a quarter. Researchers at UC Davis sequenced its 4.6-billion-base-pair genome - the first from this spider family - and found 52 chromosomes, with roughly 77% of its DNA made up of repeated sequences. The findings offer new clues about how spider chromosomes evolve and could support conservation work as rising seas threaten California’s coastal-dune habitats. The spider got its name after Colbert jokingly asked scientist Jason Bond to name one after him during a 2008 comedy segment.
