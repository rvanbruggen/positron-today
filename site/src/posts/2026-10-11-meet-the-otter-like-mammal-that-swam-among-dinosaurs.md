Around 165 million years ago, Megacauda sungei swam through lakes and rivers in what is now China, hunting fish and salamanders. At about 50 centimetres long, this remarkable mammal had a beaver-like flat tail, otter-like hunting skills and teeth suited to gripping slippery prey. Exceptionally well-preserved fossils even show impressions of its fur and skin, helping researchers piece together what it looked like and how it lived. And here's the thing: studying Megacauda is also giving scientists fresh clues about the early evolution of mammals and how they chewed their food.
