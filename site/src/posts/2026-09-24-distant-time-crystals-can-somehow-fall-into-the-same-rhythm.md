So: physicists at TU Dortmund have discovered something really cool about time crystals inside semiconductors. These crystals, which keep oscillating in a steady rhythm without any external push, can actually synchronize their spins even when they're far apart. Using lasers and a weak magnetic field, the team showed that separate regions of the material can lock their oscillations together, kind of like old pendulum clocks syncing up. And here's the thing - this synchronization happens over surprisingly large distances, opening doors for new spin-based tech networks.
