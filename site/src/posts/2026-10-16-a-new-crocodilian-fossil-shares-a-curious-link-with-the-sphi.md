So: researchers have identified a new ancient crocodilian species from a 45-million-year-old rock layer in Egypt, the same formation quarried for stones used to build the Sphinx. CT scans revealed that its nearly complete skull did not belong to the gavial genus Tomistoma, as scientists had thought for more than a century. The newly named Herugavialis cairensis is also missing the tip of its snout, a striking coincidence with the Sphinx’s famously absent nose. Its discovery adds to evidence that gavials once lived across a much wider range of habitats, including the shallow seas of the ancient Mediterranean.
