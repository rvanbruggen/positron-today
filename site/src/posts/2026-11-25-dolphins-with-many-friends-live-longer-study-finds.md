So, it seems an active social life can benefit dolphins too: a new study finds that those who spend time in large, stable groups tend to live longer. The research draws on nearly 40 years of observations of bottlenose dolphins in Shark Bay, western Australia. Dolphins broaden their social circles when young and become more selective with age, while social connections can help them build bonds, share information and find a mate. Males tend to associate with larger groups than females throughout their lives.
