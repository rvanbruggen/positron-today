Over 15 years, researchers tracked 31 young Bornean orangutans and found that their mothers deliberately arranged chances for them to play with peers. The mothers began heading toward other families a day ahead and travelled up to 40 percent farther than usual, even though the youngsters played for only about 20 minutes. These meet-ups happened on just 6 percent of days, and a maternal relative was nearby on almost every occasion. The findings reveal a hidden cost of solitary living: orangutan mothers also make an effort to give their young valuable social contact.
