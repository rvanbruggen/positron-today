So, bears don’t simply sleep through the whole winter: they enter a lighter state of torpor and regularly shift position or get up. Mother bears are awake when their cubs are born, caring for them in the den until spring. Birds, meanwhile, migrate less to escape the cold than to find food and benefit from longer daylight hours. And the familiar idea that squirrels are always forgetting where they hid their nuts is another winter wildlife myth worth questioning.
