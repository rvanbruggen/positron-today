So, researchers have identified 20 fossil fragments from Xujiayao in northern China as Denisovan remains, using ancient proteins. The fossils are at least 130,000 years old and reveal a distinctive face with a low, sloping forehead, a heavy brow ridge, rectangular eye sockets and flat cheeks. The findings offer a clearer picture of Denisovans, who were first identified through DNA from a finger bone found in Siberia. Researchers say the discovery also adds to evidence that Denisovans were widespread and diverse across Asia.
