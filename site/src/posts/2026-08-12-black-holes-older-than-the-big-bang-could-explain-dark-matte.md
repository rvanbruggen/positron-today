So, researchers from the University of Portsmouth are suggesting that black holes created before the Big Bang might still exist today. These ancient black holes could serve as 'cosmic fossils' and help unravel the mysteries of dark matter, which is crucial for understanding galaxy formation. Instead of a single explosive event, they propose a cosmic 'bounce' model where the Universe expanded after contracting. If this theory holds, it could answer some of the biggest questions in cosmology and reshape our understanding of the Universe's beginnings.
