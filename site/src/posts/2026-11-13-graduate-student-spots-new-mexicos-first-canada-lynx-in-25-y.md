In June, New Mexico State University graduate student William Grooms was reviewing wildlife-camera images from Carson National Forest when he spotted a Canada lynx. The sighting, confirmed by other researchers, is the first documented in New Mexico in about 25 years. Distinctive ear tufts and a solid-black tail tip helped distinguish the elusive cat from a bobcat. The discovery adds valuable evidence to research on how forest thinning, prescribed burns, and timber harvesting affect wildlife habitat in the Southern Rocky Mountains.
