Hannah Earley is revolutionizing the way we think about computer chips by teaching them to recycle energy that would otherwise be wasted as heat. Her startup, Vaire Computing, is developing chips that use a concept called reversible computing, which could significantly boost energy efficiency in data centers and personal devices. By retaining information during calculations, these chips can recover energy, much like keeping momentum in a race. It's an exciting time for this technology, as Earley's innovative approach has already shown promising results, proving that the idea can indeed come to life.
