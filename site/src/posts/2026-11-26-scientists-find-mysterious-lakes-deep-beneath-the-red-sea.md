Well, this is a remarkable find: researchers have discovered rare brine pools more than a mile beneath the Red Sea in the Gulf of Aqaba. Despite their oxygen-free, intensely salty waters, the pools host thriving microbial communities, offering clues about the limits of life on Earth and where scientists might look for life beyond it. Their chemistry may also preserve records of ancient tsunamis, floods, and earthquakes, making them valuable archives of the region’s geological past. And here’s the thing: these hidden underwater lakes were spotted in the final five minutes of a ten-hour robotic dive.
