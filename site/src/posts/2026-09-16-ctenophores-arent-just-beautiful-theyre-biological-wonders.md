So here's the thing - comb jellies, or ctenophores, are these glowing gelatinous creatures that split off from other animals around 700 million years ago, and they're helping us answer some of the biggest questions in biology. Scientists have discovered that these organisms, not sponges as previously thought, may represent the earliest branching animal lineage, which completely reshapes what we assumed about how the first animals looked and evolved. What fascinates me most is that ctenophores have muscles and a simple nervous system, meaning early animal life was more complex than anyone expected - they're revealing the origins of everything from bioluminescence to how brains first developed. Different species live in wildly different environments, from deep ocean pressure zones to warm coastal waters, giving researchers a natural laboratory to study how nervous systems, light-sensing genes, and other traits evolved over millions of years.
