So, the chemistry Nobel laureates Henri Kagan and Kenso Soai have developed ways to produce organic molecules in just one of two mirror-image forms. That chemical selectivity offers clues to a pivotal choice made when life emerged on Earth around four billion years ago. And here’s the thing: producing the right molecular form is also vital for safer medicines, since mirror-image molecules can have very different effects. The article also traces the field back to Louis Pasteur, whose 19th-century experiments with tartaric acid crystals revealed the phenomenon.
