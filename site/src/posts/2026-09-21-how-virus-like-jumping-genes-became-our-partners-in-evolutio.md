I find it absolutely fascinating that nearly half of our genome is made up of these wild, mobile DNA sequences called transposons. For a long time, we thought they were just junk or parasites, but it turns out they are actually key players in our evolutionary toolkit. Barbara McClintock first discovered these jumping genes decades ago, and now we know they help drive everything from new adaptations to complex biological processes. It is a brilliant example of coevolution, where these little genetic acrobats have become essential partners in our own biology. So: our genome is far more dynamic and collaborative than we ever imagined.
