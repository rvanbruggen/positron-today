So, tiny cosmic particles are constantly passing through Earth, and now we can actually detect them - which is pretty cool when you think about it. A physicist named Spencer Axani developed CosmicWatch, a device roughly the size of a box of animal crackers that costs just around $100 to build, and it flashes whenever a muon - a secondary particle created when cosmic rays hit our atmosphere - passes through it. What's really exciting is that this technology has gone from being an educational tool for high school students to being used by professional researchers studying everything from supernovae to the Great Pyramid of Giza. And here's the thing: by making muon detection affordable and portable, Axani has opened the door to experiments and outreach opportunities that were simply too expensive before.
