Choosing between two treats feels very different from taking the only one available, but new research finds a surprising similarity in how the brain prepares both decisions. When volunteers chose freely between two colored balloons or were given just one option, brain activity rose towards the same peak before they made their choice. The signal climbed faster for quick decisions and more slowly when people took their time, fitting the idea that the brain accumulates evidence as a decision forms. And here's the thing: this similarity does not by itself prove that free will is an illusion - it offers a fresh way to explore how our choices take shape.
