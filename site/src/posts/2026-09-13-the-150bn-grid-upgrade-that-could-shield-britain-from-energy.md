Britain is undertaking one of its largest electricity grid overhauls ever - a massive project that will see five times more infrastructure built by 2030 than in the previous three decades. The £150bn investment in new pylons, subsea cables, and converter stations is designed to connect growing renewable energy sources and protect UK households from the volatile energy prices that have surged since global shocks like Russia's Ukraine invasion. While these upgrades will eventually reduce costs by cutting reliance on expensive gas, the transition is being paid for through household energy bills that are already rising sharply. The scale of the project - requiring over 4,000 miles of new power lines by 2041 - has sparked protests from rural communities concerned about the infrastructure's visual impact, even as energy experts warn that failing to invest now would leave Britain even more vulnerable to future price shocks.
