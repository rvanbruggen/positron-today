As quantum computers tackle bigger problems, checking their answers with conventional computers is becoming increasingly difficult. Now, an international research team has developed a way to use experimental measurements to model how a quantum simulator behaves and estimate how uncertainty affects its results. They tested the approach first on 10 trapped ions, then demonstrated it on a 51-ion simulator in Innsbruck. And here's the thing: instead of offering a result alone, this method also gives researchers error margins that help them judge how much to trust it.
