What was once a dumping ground for material dredged from the Manchester Ship Canal has been transformed into Woolston Eyes Nature Reserve, a thriving wetland habitat that now hosts nearly two-thirds of all breeding black-necked grebes in the UK. Since intensive conservation work began in 2021, the reserve has seen remarkable growth - from just 4 nesting pairs in 2023 to 35 pairs by 2025, with most settling on newly restored islands designed specifically for them. These elegant waterfowl with their distinctive ruby-red eyes and golden feathers nest strategically near black-headed gulls for protection, creating a beautiful example of how different species can benefit from the same habitat. The reserve has become a sanctuary not just for grebes but for numerous other species of concern in the UK, including willow tits, grasshopper warblers, and pochards, showing how nature thrives when we give it a real chance.
