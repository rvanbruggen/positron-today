So, a 47,000-acre refuge off Point Lay, Alaska, will protect a vital resting place for Pacific walruses. The barrier islands and nearby Kasegaluk Lagoon are home to some of North America’s largest walrus gatherings. Around 200,000 walruses began hauling out on the islands in 2007, after Arctic sea ice became less reliable, and they have returned each year since. A conservation agreement will guard the habitat from disturbance while supporting Indigenous communities’ cultural traditions and subsistence use.
