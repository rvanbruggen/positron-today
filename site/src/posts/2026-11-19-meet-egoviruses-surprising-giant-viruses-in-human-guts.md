So, researchers have uncovered a previously unknown group of giant viruses in gut samples from people and several animal species. Their findings suggest that one group may be present in around 1% of people worldwide, meaning as many as 180 million people could carry these egoviruses. The viruses might help explain how giant viruses that infect single-celled organisms are related to poxviruses, although that evolutionary link is still a possibility rather than a proven fact. And here's the thing: their wide distribution and potential microbial hosts open up exciting new questions about the hidden diversity of life in our guts.
