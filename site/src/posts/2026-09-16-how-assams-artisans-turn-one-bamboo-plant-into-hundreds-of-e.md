So here's what fascinates me - India has 136 bamboo species, with 125 being indigenous, making it the world's second richest country in bamboo diversity, yet for centuries people walked past these plants barely noticing them. In Jorhat, Assam, craftsperson Prabhat Saikia shows us what's possible when you really look closer - turning a humble bamboo pole into furniture, homes, food containers, musical instruments and even textiles. What makes this work isn't just the plant's natural versatility, but the skills and proper treatment techniques Saikia learned from working with the Rainforest Research Institute. And here's the thing - his workshop now employs around 20 young artisans who've found dignified livelihoods without leaving their villages, proving that sustainable materials paired with knowledge can transform both lives and communities.
