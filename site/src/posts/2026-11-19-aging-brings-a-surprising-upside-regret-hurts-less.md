Here’s a hopeful finding about aging: although younger and older adults report a similar number of regrets, older people tend to feel less upset by their recent ones. In a study of adults aged 21 to 89, researchers found that the emotional sting of regret often softens with age. Regrets about missed opportunities can still linger, though, since we can learn from choices that went wrong but can’t revisit chances we never took. So, as we get older, regret may become less about dwelling on the past and more about finding meaning in it - a lovely nudge to make the most of opportunities today.
