The remote Pacific atolls of Bikar and Bokak are among the most intact ecosystems left on Earth, with remarkably little human influence. A new study documented thriving seabird colonies, nesting turtles, abundant reef fish and diverse deep-sea communities, including species not previously recorded in the Marshall Islands. The country has now designated about 18,500 square miles around the atolls as its first national marine sanctuary. It’s a wonderful step that will help protect these biodiversity hotspots and preserve a valuable reference for conservation around the world.
