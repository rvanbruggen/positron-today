With their long bills and cartoonish mating calls, woodcocks are distinctive birds - and their evolutionary history is just as surprising. By analyzing DNA from all eight woodcock species and 22 snipes, researchers traced how their ancestors spread from the Northern Hemisphere to islands and warmer regions around the world. The study also showed that a fossil from Haiti once thought to be a woodcock was actually a snipe. Many woodcocks now live only on particular islands, making the protection of their forest habitats especially important as climates change.
