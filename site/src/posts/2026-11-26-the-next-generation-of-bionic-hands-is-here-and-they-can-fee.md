So, a new bionic hand is giving Avi Davidson something remarkable: the ability to feel pressure through his prosthetic. Connected to nerves in his forearm, the experimental LUKE arm helps him handle delicate things like grapes, eggs, and a loved one’s hand. Davidson is the ninth person in a University of Utah trial to receive the implant, and the first to take the device home and use it independently. Researchers hope his experience will show how the technology fits into daily life and what it can mean for users’ well-being and nervous systems.
