Female mosquitoes find human hosts through several sensory systems, and ionotropic receptors help them detect the acidic compounds that give human sweat its sour smell. So, researchers have revealed structures of a receptor complex from Anopheles stephensi, a mosquito that spreads malaria. The structures show that when an acid binds, the receptor opens an ion-conducting pore and sends an electrical signal that helps guide the mosquito towards people. And here's the exciting possibility: understanding this process could inspire new ways to make humans less attractive to malaria-carrying mosquitoes.
