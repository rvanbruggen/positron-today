So, why do cancer-associated PIK3CA mutations cause abnormal growth in some blood vessels but rarely in arteries? These mutations can lead to malformations in veins, capillaries and lymphatic vessels, sometimes from birth and sometimes later in life. Sabata and colleagues investigate this intriguing difference. The key, they find, lies in the identity and maturity of the cells affected by the mutations.
