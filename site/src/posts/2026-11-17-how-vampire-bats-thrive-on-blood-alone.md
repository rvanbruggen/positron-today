So, how does a bat make a living from a meal that is mostly water, low in nutrients and rich in iron? A new comparison of vampire-bat genomes and gene activity suggests their stomachs have evolved into stretchy, neutral-pH sacs that store blood rather than digest it with acid. After a feast, activity of a fluid-transporting gene in the stomach rises more than 900-fold, helping the bats shed water quickly and take flight. The findings also reveal genes they have retained or lost as they adapted to this remarkable blood-only diet.
