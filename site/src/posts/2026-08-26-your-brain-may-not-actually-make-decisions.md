Isn't it fascinating to think that our brains might not actually 'make' decisions in the way we assume? Professor Tom James from Indiana University challenges the traditional view of decision-making as a linear process. Instead, he suggests that our actions emerge from a complex interplay of sensory and motor processes, rather than a distinct cognitive stage. This idea reshapes how we understand behavior, emphasizing that decisions might just be abstract descriptions rather than direct causes of our actions.
