So, researchers have directly observed electrons in graphene making quantum jumps across energy gaps that are normally forbidden. A new technique combining laser pulses and a photoemission momentum microscope acts like a slow-motion camera, tracking how intense electric fields move electrons between energy bands. The exceptionally pure, one-atom-thick graphene samples helped the team capture this ultrafast process in real time. These detailed observations could help scientists understand how such transitions may matter for high-speed semiconductor electronics.
