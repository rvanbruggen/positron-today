So: tropical trees from the genus Clusia have a fascinating trick to survive drought by breathing at night. Researchers discovered that ancient genome duplications and reshuffling helped these trees evolve a special type of photosynthesis called CAM, which lets them absorb CO2 at night instead of during the day. Each species uses CAM differently, showing a remarkable diversity in how they manage water and carbon. And here's the thing - this discovery not only solves a 200-year-old mystery but also highlights the incredible adaptability of plants in tough environments.
