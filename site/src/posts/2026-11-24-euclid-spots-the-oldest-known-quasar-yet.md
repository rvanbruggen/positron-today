So: Euclid has spotted a record-breaking quasar, formed just 630 million years after the Big Bang, when the universe was only five percent of its current age. Named Dún Aonghasa, the object is so far away that we see it as it appeared about 13.1 billion years ago. Its supermassive black hole weighs roughly 360 million suns, yet the quasar is feeding relatively slowly - a puzzle that challenges ideas about how black holes grew so quickly in the early universe. And here's the exciting part: Euclid has already found 31 quasars, and astronomers expect it to uncover more from this ancient era.
