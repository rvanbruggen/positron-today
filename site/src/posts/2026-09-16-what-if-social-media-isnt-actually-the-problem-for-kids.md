Psychology researcher Peter Gray is challenging Jonathan Haidt's hugely influential theory that social media is destroying teen mental health - and it's a fascinating counterpoint to a narrative that's already shaped policy worldwide. Gray argues that decades of reduced childhood autonomy, combined with rigid schooling methods and the Common Core curriculum, are the real culprits behind rising anxiety in teens. He actually points out that internet adoption in the 1990s and 2000s initially improved teen wellbeing by restoring freedom and connection, and that studies consistently show kids identify school pressure - not social media - as their top source of stress. So here's the thing: Gray worries that social media bans might become a new harm themselves, distracting from solutions that could actually make a difference.
