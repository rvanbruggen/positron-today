So, an Australian study found that zoo lions behaved more like their wild counterparts when whole carcasses were added to a feast-and-fast feeding routine. Over three months, three adult male lions spent almost three times longer eating on carcass-feeding days, then rested more and gradually became more active as the fasting period continued. Their pattern of feasting, resting and renewed activity closely resembled the natural rhythm seen in wild lions. Pacing appeared more connected to familiar routines and keeper visits than to hunger, and researchers observed no rise in aggression.
