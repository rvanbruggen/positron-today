So here's what's fascinating: an 18-year-old student from Philadelphia has actually cracked something that's stumped musicologists for centuries - what makes Mozart's music so distinctively brilliant. Linus Chen-Plotkin analysed 600 movements from Mozart, Haydn, Beethoven and Schubert using statistical software and Markov chains, and discovered that Mozart's melodies have a shorter "memory" - meaning they're more surprising and unpredictable moment-to-moment. His work shows that Mozart sets up patterns of expectation in his music and then confounds them, which is exactly what gives those pieces their dizzying richness and magic. What I find really encouraging is that Chen-Plotkin isn't arguing maths replaces musicology - he's simply showing how data can reveal the patterns underlying human creativity, and his work is just getting started.
