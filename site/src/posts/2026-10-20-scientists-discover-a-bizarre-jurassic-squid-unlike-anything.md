So, two exceptionally rare fossils from Wyoming have revealed Wyoteuthis linsterorum, a squid relative that lived around 160 million years ago. Its barrel-shaped internal skeleton is about 10 centimetres long and an extraordinary 6 centimetres wide, making it the chunkiest known belemnite. CT scans also uncovered a surprising link to an ancient belemnite family thought to have vanished millions of years earlier. Researchers suggest its impressive size may have helped it hunt smaller belemnites, and estimate the animal could have reached about 60 centimetres from arms to body.
