When road-widening threatened mature trees in Kozhikode, Kerala, researchers rescued the native orchids growing on them and relocated the plants to three nearby sites. A study published in Plants found that 19,511 of 27,324 orchids across 21 species survived after 12 to 24 months - an encouraging 71.4%. Survival was strongest at sheltered Sarovaram Biopark, while windier, saltier Kappad Beach recorded the highest flowering rate among survivors. Although regionally endemic orchids had a lower survival rate of 55%, some high-altitude and mist-forest species also survived and reproduced after relocation, offering a hopeful lesson in conservation.
