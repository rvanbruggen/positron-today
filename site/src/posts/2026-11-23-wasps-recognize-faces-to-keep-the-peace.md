So, paper wasps can recognize one another by spotting differences in facial color patterns, thanks to specialized neurons in their tiny brains. For female northern paper wasps, remembering who’s who helps maintain the nest’s social hierarchy and avoid repeated battles over dominance. They can even watch a fight and later treat the winner and loser differently. Studying this surprisingly primate-like ability could also help researchers better understand how our own brains recognize individuals.
