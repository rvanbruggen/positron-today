Here’s a clever twist on solar power: prickly pears use a special form of photosynthesis that creates a pH difference the researchers can harness to generate electricity. A team at UC Santa Cruz found that linking just eight plants can produce 4.5 volts, enough to power low-energy sensors that monitor crops. And here's the exciting part: prickly pears kept photosynthesizing and generating electricity even after 15 months without water. Other plants that use this process, including aloe, pineapple, and agave, could also help power resilient farm sensors.
