For over 70 years, the leukemia drug 6-thioguanine (6-TG) has been used effectively, but scientists have now uncovered a surprising factor influencing its action: the protein NUDT5. Unlike what was expected, it’s not NUDT5’s enzymatic activity that matters, but the presence of the protein itself that affects how cells respond to the drug. Researchers developed new chemical degraders to remove NUDT5, revealing that cells without it resist 6-TG’s toxic effects. This discovery opens fresh perspectives on how leukemia treatments work and could lead to improved therapies.
