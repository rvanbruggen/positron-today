So, a human-made lagoon on the coast of the United Arab Emirates has become a vital refuge for young green turtles. Researchers tracking nine turtles found they spent more than 80 percent of their time in the protected wetland, where algae likely provides plenty to eat. The natural mangrove channels are also essential, helping turtles move between the lagoon and the sea. These findings show how engineered wetlands can support wildlife when they remain connected to nature and are protected from human pressures.
