A Czech archaeological mission has returned to tombs at Saqqara first explored by Auguste Mariette in 1858–1860, uncovering features buried again for more than 160 years. Researchers relocated the vast tomb of Ptahshepses, a senior official who served four Fifth Dynasty kings, and found his remains inside a limestone sarcophagus. Analysis indicates he lived beyond 60, an exceptional age in ancient Egypt, while the newly identified chapel of his wife Ikhetneferet reveals more about the family. Together, the reliefs and burial finds are enriching understanding of Old Kingdom elites, family ties and mummification, with excavations set to continue.
