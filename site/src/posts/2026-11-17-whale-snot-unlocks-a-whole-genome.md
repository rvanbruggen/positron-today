Scientists have used drones to collect DNA-rich blow, or “whale snot,” from humpbacks off British Columbia and sequence whole genomes. The method keeps researchers farther from the animals than traditional skin biopsies, and some samples were gathered from land without a boat. Genetic diversity estimates from the blow matched those from biopsies, while the studied population showed little sign of recent inbreeding. The new genomes will help researchers track population health, structure, and family ties among North Pacific humpbacks.
