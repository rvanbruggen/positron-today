Now, a four-year trial in Belo Horizonte, Brazil, has shown that releasing mosquitoes carrying Wolbachia bacteria can help protect communities from mosquito-borne diseases. Among schoolchildren in areas receiving the mosquitoes, dengue infections fell by 68.5% and chikungunya infections by 73.3%, even during Brazil’s largest recorded dengue outbreak. The bacteria pass from mosquitoes to their offspring and make it harder for them to spread viruses. And here's the thing: the results add to growing evidence that this approach could become a powerful tool against dengue worldwide, while there were too few Zika cases to assess its effect.
