In the 1930s, a group of university students in Budapest, including the brilliant Paul Erdős, gathered to discuss math and share ideas. One day, Esther Klein presented a fascinating puzzle about five dots on a page, leading to a breakthrough in understanding convex shapes. It turns out that no matter how you arrange the dots, four of them will always form a convex quadrilateral! This discovery not only advanced mathematical theory but also sparked a lifelong romance for Klein. It's amazing how a simple question can lead to such profound impacts in both math and life.
