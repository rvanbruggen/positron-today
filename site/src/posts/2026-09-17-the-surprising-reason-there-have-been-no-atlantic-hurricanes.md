So here's what's fascinating - we're looking at something genuinely rare happening in the Atlantic right now, with zero hurricanes recorded so far this year. In the last 175 years of records, this has only occurred twice before, back in 1907 and 1914. Now, El Niño tends to get all the attention when we talk about hurricane suppression, but it turns out there's another force at play here that's contributing to fewer storms and pushing the season peak later than usual. And here's the thing - we're even seeing intensified drought conditions across the Caribbean islands partly because of this lack of tropical storms.
