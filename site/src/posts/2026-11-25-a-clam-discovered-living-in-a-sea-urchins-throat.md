So, researchers exploring the deep seafloor of the Great Australian Bight found a new clam species living inside a sea urchin’s oesophagus, or throat. The tiny clam, named Vaticinaria gulicola, was discovered between about 13,000 and 16,400 feet beneath the surface, where sunlight never reaches. Even more fascinating, related clams had been hiding in a sea urchin specimen collected during the 19th-century Challenger Expedition, unnoticed for more than a century. How the clam lives inside its host and what it eats are still mysteries, making this an exciting discovery for future deep-sea research.
