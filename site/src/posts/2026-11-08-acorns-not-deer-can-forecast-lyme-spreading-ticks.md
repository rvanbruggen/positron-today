So, here's a surprising clue to future Lyme disease risk: the number of acorns on the forest floor. A 35-year study in New York found that abundant red oak acorns help white-footed mice survive and thrive, giving tick larvae more chances to become nymphs that can bite people. Researchers say acorn production can help predict especially risky years almost two years ahead, though deer remain essential to tick populations. Warmer weather complicates the picture, and its overall effect on Lyme risk is still uncertain.
