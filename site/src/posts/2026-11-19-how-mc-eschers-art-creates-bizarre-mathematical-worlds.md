So, here’s the fascinating twist: M.C. Escher struggled with maths at school, yet his art became a lasting source of inspiration for mathematicians. His fascination with patterns at Granada’s Alhambra led him to explore how repeating shapes could fill a surface, and he developed striking tessellations featuring creatures like fish, birds and lizards. His intuitive discoveries about symmetry caught the attention of mathematicians and crystallographers, while his exchange with geometer H.S.M. Coxeter helped inspire some of his most complex work. A new exhibition at New York’s National Museum of Mathematics celebrates this creative journey through February 8, 2027.
