So: AI data centres use huge amounts of electricity, and their rapid growth is putting pressure on energy systems around the world. At the University of Southern Denmark, engineers are capturing all the heat from the Bitten supercomputer with liquid cooling instead of letting it escape into the air. The recovered hot water is fed into Sonderborg’s district heating network, helping warm homes and potentially supporting industrial processes too. It’s a practical way to turn a growing energy challenge into a useful local resource.
