When dogs hear the word 'no' and restrain their behavior, their brains light up in ways that look remarkably like human self-control - a discovery that fascinates scientists studying animal cognition. Researchers used non-invasive EEG scans on 14 dogs to measure brain activity during commands, finding that the same theta waves associated with human willpower appear in dogs' frontal lobes. This pattern suggests that dogs engage in conscious self-control similar to humans, not just automatic reflexes. The findings could help scientists better understand human conditions like ADHD and obesity by studying how dogs' brains handle impulse control.
