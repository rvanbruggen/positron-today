So, Euclid’s first images offer a spectacular look at millions of galaxies, from the Milky Way’s wispy outer reaches to distant spiral worlds. With a field of view around a hundred times larger than the James Webb Space Telescope’s, Euclid can map vast stretches of sky in remarkable detail. Over six years, it aims to photograph more than 1.5 billion galaxies and build a three-dimensional map of the universe. And here’s the thing: that cosmic atlas could help scientists uncover the mysteries of dark matter and dark energy.
