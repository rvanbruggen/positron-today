That fleshy flap above a turkey’s beak is called a snood, and it can swell and lengthen when a male is showing off. Research by biologist Richard Buchholz suggests it helps attract mates: female turkeys tend to prefer males with longer snoods, which may signal better health and resistance to parasites. Snoods also appear to help males size up rivals, with longer-snooded birds more likely to hold their ground over food. So: this unusual feature may be both a signal of good genes and a handy badge of status.
