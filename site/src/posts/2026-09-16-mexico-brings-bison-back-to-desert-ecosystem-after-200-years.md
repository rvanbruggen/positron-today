After two centuries away, bison have returned to Mexico's Chihuahua Desert - and it's a game-changer for the landscape. Forty-eight of these magnificent animals were brought to El Santuario Wildlife Reserve in Cuatrociénegas last year, where they're already working their ecological magic on the sensitive arid grasslands. So here's the thing: bison are basically nature's landscape managers - their hooves break up soil, their coats spread seeds, their dung fertilizes, and they shape the terrain just by being themselves. This reintroduction isn't just about bringing back an iconic species; it's about restoring balance and memory to an ecosystem that's been without them for far too long, giving real hope for sustainable land management.
