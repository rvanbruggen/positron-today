So, a DNA variant inherited from Neanderthals is linked to a smaller cuneus, a visual-processing region at the back of the human brain. Researchers found it in all five sequenced Neanderthal genomes, but not in Denisovan genomes, and today it appears only in people with non-African ancestry. Each copy was associated with about 0.85 cubic centimetres less cuneus volume - a remarkably large effect compared with other known variants. And here's the intriguing twist: carriers also have a lower risk of schizophrenia, although the finding does not show that the variant itself causes this difference.
