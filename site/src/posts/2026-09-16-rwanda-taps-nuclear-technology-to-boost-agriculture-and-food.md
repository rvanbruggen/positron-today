Rwanda is diving into nuclear and isotopic techniques to strengthen agricultural productivity, food safety and climate resilience - which I think is a really smart move for a country serious about long-term food security. The government has formally expressed interest in joining the Atoms4Food initiative, backed by the IAEA and UN's Food and Agriculture Organization, which supports countries in using nuclear technology to boost crop yields, manage resources and reduce food losses. This fits into Rwanda's broader nuclear programme that already includes plans for nuclear power and a research reactor, with the country having hosted multiple IAEA missions to assess its readiness. What's particularly impressive is how Rwanda is building the human and institutional capacity needed to sustain this work through training, education and international partnerships - they're clearly thinking long-term.
