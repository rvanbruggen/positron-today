Denmark is launching Europe's first large-scale CO2 storage facility in Esbjerg, where a specially designed ship will transport 400,000 tons of CO2 annually to an old oil field in the North Sea for underground storage. The project represents a crucial step in helping European industries reduce emissions while staying competitive, with Denmark investing 16 billion kroner to support this carbon capture and storage technology. Rather than closing factories, this approach allows companies to keep producing while permanently locking away greenhouse gases that would otherwise reach the atmosphere. The British oil company Ineos is leading the initiative, initially capturing CO2 from Danish biogas plants and eventually expanding to capture emissions directly from factory smokestacks once facilities are installed.
