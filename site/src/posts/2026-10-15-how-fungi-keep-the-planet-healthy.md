So, beneath our feet, mycorrhizal fungi are quietly trading minerals with plant roots through vast underground networks. These partnerships enrich soil and capture about 13 billion tons of carbon dioxide each year, while connecting with roughly 80% of plant species on Earth. The fascinating twist is that fungi with no brains can still act strategically, shifting resources toward plants that offer a better deal. Evolutionary biologist Toby Kiers is uncovering how this hidden trade works and bringing researchers together to map and protect these vital networks.
