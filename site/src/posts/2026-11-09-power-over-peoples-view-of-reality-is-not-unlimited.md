Åsa Wikforss explores how democratic values can grow even as authoritarian politics gain ground around the world. She explains that would-be autocrats often keep elections while weakening the media, public institutions and their opponents, making it harder for voters to recognise the shift. But recent election defeats for authoritarian leaders and movements in Hungary, Serbia and Brazil show that this model is not unstoppable. When independent voices reach people and voters unite, democratic rights can still make a real difference.
