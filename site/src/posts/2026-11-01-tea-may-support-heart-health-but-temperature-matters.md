So, a large study following nearly 486,000 adults in China found that regular tea drinking was associated with an 11% lower risk of several vascular diseases. The lowest estimated risk appeared among people drinking around six to eight cups a day, though the study cannot show that tea itself caused the difference. Black tea and moderately strong tea showed more consistent links with lower risk than other tea habits. And here's the surprising catch: drinking tea piping hot was associated with higher risk, so letting it cool a little may be a sensible choice.
