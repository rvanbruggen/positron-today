Here’s a thrilling glimpse of the Universe’s infancy: Euclid has uncovered 31 exceptionally ancient quasars, powered by enormous black holes. Two set new records, with redshifts of 7.77 and 7.69. They were already shining as brightly as a trillion Suns when the Universe was just 670 million years old. By finding fainter quasars across vast areas of sky, Euclid is giving astronomers a fuller picture of these cosmic beacons and how the first galaxies and black holes grew so quickly.
