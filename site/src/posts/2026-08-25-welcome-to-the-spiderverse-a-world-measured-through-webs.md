Counting creatures in our environment is vital for conservation efforts, and recent advances in eco-surveillance are making this task easier. Scientists are now using environmental DNA, or eDNA, found in spiderwebs to monitor biodiversity and detect invasive species. This sticky material captures genetic material from various organisms, providing a rich source of data. Interestingly, researchers are even experimenting with synthetic webs to enhance this process further, making it more efficient and less harmful to the ecosystem.
