So here's this remarkable moment - the Azcatitlan Codex, a 50-folio treasure documenting Aztec history, has finally come home to Mexico after nearly 180 years away in France. The manuscript left Mexico back in 1840 and now it's on loan at the National Museum of Anthropology in Mexico City for three months, thanks to an agreement between French president Macron and Mexican president Sheinbaum celebrating 200 years of diplomatic ties. This late 17th-century document tells the story of pre-Hispanic peoples' journey from Aztlán to founding Tenochtitlan, right up through the Spanish conquest - and I find it fascinating that it was drawn on European paper in color, mixing Indigenous knowledge with European techniques. What really gets me is how this codex has travelled through history, passing through scholars' hands, Jesuit libraries, and eventually to a French collector before ending up in the Bibliothèque Nationale de France - and now, finally, Mexicans can see their own heritage up close again.
