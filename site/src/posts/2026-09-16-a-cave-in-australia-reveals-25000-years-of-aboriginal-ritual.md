Researchers have uncovered compelling evidence that Cloggs Cave, a sacred site for the GunaiKurnai people, was used for spiritual rituals and ceremonies for roughly 25,000 years. By studying microscopic silica structures called phytoliths from burnt plant remains, scientists discovered that ancestors deliberately carried specific grasses into the cave and burned them in carefully layered ash deposits. The upper sections contained 73 distinct ash layers created between 4,400 and 1,600 years ago, and researchers even found a standing stone intentionally placed inside the cave around 2,000 years ago for ritual purposes. This discovery, made in close collaboration with GunaiKurnai representatives throughout the research process, offers a fascinating window into how Aboriginal communities practiced healing, magic, and spiritual ceremonies across millennia.
