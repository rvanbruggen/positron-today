Maharashtra has become India's solar pump leader, with over 747,000 installations already in place and a target of 1.045 million - even setting a Guinness World Record by installing nearly 46,000 pumps in just 30 days. What makes this transformation so remarkable is that farmers like those in Dawwa village can now irrigate their fields during daylight hours, powered entirely by the sun, without the stress of erratic electricity grids or rising diesel costs. Government schemes like PM-KUSUM and Maharashtra's own MTSKPY have made solar irrigation affordable for ordinary smallholders through subsidies and reduced pump prices, turning what was once a luxury into something within reach. Across India, states like Kerala, Chhattisgarh, and Assam are following similar paths, proving that solar-powered agriculture isn't just environmentally smart - it's economically practical for farmers with thin margins.
