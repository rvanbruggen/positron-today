Now, here's a lovely surprise: ESA's Euclid telescope, designed to study distant galaxies and dark matter, has discovered its first previously unknown comet. Specialized software spotted the moving streak in images taken from September 16 to 24, and astronomers confirmed it as C/2026 S1 (Euclid) on October 8. About 1.3 billion kilometers from Earth, the comet follows an unusual path far above the plane of the planets, on a journey that takes thousands of years. And here's the exciting part: Euclid can spot comets while they are still far from the Sun, potentially helping ESA find a future target for its Comet Interceptor mission.
