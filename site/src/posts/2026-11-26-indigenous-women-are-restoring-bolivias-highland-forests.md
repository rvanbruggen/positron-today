So: Indigenous Quechua communities in Bolivia’s Tunari mountains are bringing back native Kewiña trees, after decades of deforestation left only a fraction of the original forest. With support from the nonprofit Faunagua, community members have grown and planted nearly 500,000 seedlings to help restore watersheds and the water sources they sustain. The initiative also supports women’s education and financial independence, helping communities shape a more lasting future for their forests. The trees grow slowly, so the project’s long-term impact remains to be seen, but this community-led work is already an encouraging step toward healthier landscapes.
