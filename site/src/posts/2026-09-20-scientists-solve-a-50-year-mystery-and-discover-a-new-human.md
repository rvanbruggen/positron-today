Researchers at NHS Blood and Transplant in Bristol have finally solved a puzzle that's been hanging around since 1972 - they've traced the mysterious AnWj antigen marker on red blood cells back to its genetic source. What they found was that the MAL gene produces a protein called Mal that's responsible for the AnWj antigen, and they proved it by testing in laboratory conditions. This breakthrough means MAL is now officially recognized as a human blood group system, making it ISBT 047. So here's the thing - while over 99.9% of people have this antigen, the tiny minority who don't can face serious transfusion reactions if they receive AnWj positive blood, and now doctors have a clear way to identify and protect these exceptionally rare patients.
