So, the Rhine may have looked like a boundary 5,000 years ago, but new archaeological research suggests it didn’t keep communities apart. Simple pottery gradually spread across wide regions, while distinctive double axes point to a shared idea linking France, Central Europe and Scandinavia. Communities also built large collective tombs with common features, even as local traditions shaped their design. I love how these finds reveal a connected network that made room for both shared ideas and regional identities.
