So, in Jordan, researchers from eight countries with difficult relations share SESAME, a powerful synchrotron that helps them study everything from ancient papyrus to medicines. The machine grew from a discarded accelerator in Berlin into a remarkable regional research facility, powered in part by its own solar park. Its political significance is striking, but the article suggests that the real magic lies in researchers simply working together on concrete scientific questions. Shared curiosity, it seems, can create cooperation without anyone needing to begin with grand promises of peace.
