Isn't it fascinating to think that humans might share a common ancestor with a tiny, one-eyed creature? This research reveals that nearly 600 million years ago, our vertebrate ancestors had a cyclops-like stage. Over time, as they adapted to a more mobile lifestyle, they developed paired eyes from a central visual structure. This unexpected journey in eye evolution helps explain why vertebrate eyes are so different from those of insects and squid.
