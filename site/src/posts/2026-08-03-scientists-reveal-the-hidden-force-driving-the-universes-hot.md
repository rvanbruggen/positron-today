When atomic nuclei collide at nearly light speed, they create quark-gluon plasma, a super-hot state of matter. This research dives into how acceleration within this plasma contributes to its rapid expansion, which is just as crucial as its swirling motion. The team from Fudan University has mapped acceleration across various collision energies, revealing fascinating insights about the plasma's behavior. This could even lead to a new axis in the phase structure of QCD matter, opening doors to explore previously uncharted phenomena.
