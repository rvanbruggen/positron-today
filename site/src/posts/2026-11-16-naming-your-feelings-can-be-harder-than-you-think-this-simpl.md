Putting a name to a feeling can be surprisingly difficult: around one in 10 people struggle significantly to identify their emotions. The feelings wheel, created by psychologist Gloria Willcox in the early 1980s, helps users move from broad labels such as “sad” to more precise descriptions. This skill, known as emotional granularity, can offer clues about why we feel a certain way and what might help, and research links it with better mental health and social functioning. Experts are still exploring exactly why, but more specific emotional words can give us useful information for responding to what we need.
