Renata Flores has become a global voice for Quechua, blending the Indigenous language of the Incas with rap, trap and Andean instruments. She first reached a wide audience as a teenager with a Quechua cover of a Michael Jackson hit, and says singing in the language gives her a strong sense of identity and freedom. Now her music also speaks out about discrimination, women’s rights and political violence, while her forthcoming album carries a message of resilience: “we are still here.” And here's the thing: Flores has helped inspire a lively new generation of Quechua-speaking artists in Ayacucho to reclaim their language with pride.
